What a different pipe size changes

Pipe (OD × wall)ID mmSpeed m/sFriction barFlow m³/hNet barJet m/sPower kWWall MPa

Same nozzles, water level, frequency and valve as the inputs. A bigger inside diameter cuts friction sharply (friction goes with diameter to the power of about five), so more flow reaches the nozzles until the pump runs out. A smaller pipe does the reverse. Wall MPa is the pipe wall stress at the worst case (pump shut-off plus gravity help).

What changing the pump or the stages does

PumpFlow m³/hNet barGauge barShut-off barPower kWMotor kW

Same pipe, nozzles, water level, frequency and valve as the inputs. CRN 64 stages scale your sheet's CRN 64-3 curve by stages divided by 3, which matches the datasheet's head per stage of about 30 m. CRN 90, 120 and 150 are indicative only: they scale the same curve by the nominal flow, so replace them with datasheet points before relying on them. Motor sizes are the standard P2 ratings from the Grundfos CR 64 and CR 90 datasheets, and CRN 120 and 150 motors are not filled in. Power turns red above the motor rating, and a star means the flow is outside the range of the fitted points.

What changing the pump frequency does

FrequencyFlow m³/hNet barJet m/sGauge barShut-off barPower kW

Same pipe, nozzles, water level and valve as the inputs. Lowering the frequency cuts power quickly but nozzle pressure only slowly, because the water column in the pipe keeps pushing. Shut-off is the gauge level that means blocked nozzles, and it changes with frequency. A star means the flow is outside the range of the pump points, so the row is an estimate. Power turns red above the motor rating.

The same pipe size in other pipe types

Pipe typeYield MPaAllowed barWorst-case factorBurst barCollapse barTension factor

Allowed bar is the highest wall pressure difference that keeps your design safety factor. Worst case is pump shut-off plus gravity help. Collapse is for an empty pipe under the well water outside (the lower of yield and elastic collapse). Tension is the pipe weight in air plus the tool, hanging from the top. Strength values are typical minimums for each type, so confirm them against the mill certificate. These checks do not cover couplings and thread make-up, corrosion allowance, bending, water hammer or fatigue.

MS and GI pipe: the standard 3 in (80 NB) walls are 3.2 mm (light), 4.05 mm (medium) and 4.85 mm (heavy), and the pipe is usually threaded and socketed. The thread row is an estimate for a 1.5 mm deep thread. The tube strength is the same for MS and GI, because galvanising only adds a zinc coat. Chlorine solution attacks zinc and steel, so a galvanised coat will not last, and the solution strength and flushing after the job should be checked.

Your sheet's friction scales exactly with flow squared, which means it uses one constant friction factor. Colebrook-White lets the factor change with the Reynolds number and the pipe roughness, so the two methods differ.
